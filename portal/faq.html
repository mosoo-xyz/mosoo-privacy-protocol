How does Mosoo differ from Tornado Cash?
01 +

Tornado Cash was a fixed-denomination coin tumbler (e.g. exactly 0.1, 1, 10, or 100 ETH). You could only deposit a fixed denomination and withdraw that exact same amount. It had zero private-to-private transfers, no ability to make change, and no viewing keys for compliance audits.

Mosoo is an arbitrary-amount Multi-UTXO privacy engine (architecturally similar to modern zero-knowledge UTXO privacy architectures). You can deposit arbitrary amounts (e.g., 23.45 ABEY), execute confidential peer-to-peer transfers inside the pool, receive change notes, and generate read-only Viewing Keys to share with tax authorities or auditors without sacrificing your public privacy.

Is using zero-knowledge privacy legal?
02 +

Yes. Privacy is a fundamental civil right and an absolute requirement for everyday business commerce. In the real world, no corporation publishes its employees' payroll or supplier agreements on a public billboard, and no individual wants their bank account balance visible to merchants every time they buy a coffee.

Furthermore, Mosoo includes Stealth Viewing Keys: if a lawful user needs to prove their source of funds or income to a tax agency or court, they can export a cryptographically provable read-only audit record without exposing their transactions to the public internet.

Do I need gas in my withdrawal wallet to unshield?
03 +

No, you do not. This is one of Mosoo's most critical privacy safeguards.

In traditional setups, funding a brand-new wallet with gas from an old exchange or wallet immediately links the two addresses on-chain. Mosoo uses an independent Relayer network: the relayer submits the transaction and pays the on-chain gas fee. In exchange, the smart contract deducts a small fee from the note itself. Your fresh wallet receives clean ABEY starting from a 0.00 gas balance.

Can a relayer steal my funds or modify the withdrawal address?
04 +

No, mathematically impossible. When your browser prepares a withdrawal, the destination address, amount, and fee are bound into the zero-knowledge proof or cryptographic authorizer signature.

If a malicious relayer attempts to alter even a single byte of the recipient address, the smart contract's verifyProof call evaluates to false and the transaction reverts immediately. Relayers only have the power to submit the transaction as constructed by you.

How fast are zero-knowledge proofs generated in the browser?
05 +

Thanks to the ZK-friendly Poseidon hash function, our circuits require fewer than 20,000 R1CS constraints. Generating a Groth16 witness and proof in your browser typically takes 1.5 to 3.5 seconds on a modern laptop, and 3 to 5 seconds on a modern smartphone, running entirely in client-side WebAssembly without sending sensitive data anywhere.

What happens if I lose my secret viewing key or browser storage?
06 +

Zero-knowledge cryptography is mathematically absolute: there is no password reset and no administrator backdoor. Your notes and keys are encrypted locally in your browser's SecureVault. You must back up your Viewing Key and note secrets. If you clear your browser cookies and storage without backing up your key, the funds cannot be recovered by anyone.

What are the best practices for maximizing my privacy?
07 +

To achieve the strongest operational security (OpSec):

1. Wait between shield and unshield: Allowing multiple other deposits to enter the Merkle tree deepens the anonymity set before you withdraw.
2. Avoid identical deposit/withdrawal amounts: If you deposit 100 ABEY and immediately withdraw 100 ABEY, heuristic analysts might correlate the amounts. Transacting privately or withdrawing in uneven increments breaks timing and amount correlations.
3. Never withdraw to your deposit address: Always withdraw to a fresh, unlinked wallet address.
